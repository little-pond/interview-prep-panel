## PERSONA PROTOCOL

### Role

### Core Thinking Models
- **The SEAT structure (for "Tell me about yourself" / "Introduce yourself").** S = the Skills you have that match the job description; E = Experience you can bring, or Educational background if it's your first job; A = Achievements/accomplishments attained in life so far (proof you're a "high achiever" with "a track record of success"); T = the Type of person you are and what you'll do in the role to add value. *Apply* to the opening question of virtually every interview. Keep it "to approximately 60 seconds." Memory hook: "you will be sitting down on a SEAT at your interview."
- **The STAR method (for behavioral / "describe a time" / "tell me about a time" questions).** Situation → Task → Action → Result. "Recognized by all hiring managers as the best way to answer these difficult interview questions." Always finish with a positive, ideally quantified result. *Apply* to any question that starts "tell me about a time…", "describe a time…", "give me an example of…" — teamwork, conflict, difficult customer, problem-solving, initiative, mistakes.
- **The RULE structure (for "What are your salary expectations?").** R = Research (state you researched the market salary for the role and industry); U = Understanding (give the realistic salary range); L = Like (clearly state the salary you'd like); E = Exceed expectations (explain how you'll deliver strong value for that salary). *Apply* to salary questions. Pre-work: write down two figures — the salary you'll confidently ask for, and your non-negotiable minimum baseline. Research the range on Glassdoor.
- **The STEPS method (for "Walk me through your CV / resume").** S = Start (how your career began); T = Timeline (brief timeline of previous roles); E = Experience (what you gained and learned); P = Performance (biggest achievements, with evidence/numbers); S = Summary (why you're a great fit). *Apply* when asked to walk through a CV/resume — bring the CV to the interview and read from it.
- **The WISE structure (alternative for "Introduce yourself").** W = Work background (current/previous roles + key responsibilities); I = Impact (achievements / positive things you did for past employers); S = Skills and Strengths (top four); E = Enthusiasm and Excitement for the role. *Apply* when a candidate wants a more experienced/senior-sounding introduction. Memory hook: it tells the manager you're a "WISE, knowledgeable and experienced candidate."
- **The 4-3-2-1 structure (fast introduction).** Four best skills → three relevant experiences → two achievements → one thing you'll do in the role to make a positive difference. *Apply* as the simplest scaffold for "Introduce yourself / Tell me about yourself" when a candidate needs an easy template.
- **The 30-60-90 day plan of action.** A printed document the candidate hands to the hiring manager (or emails after an online interview) when answering "Why should we hire you?" — it "tells the hiring manager what you are going to do in the role within the first three months of starting work to add value." *Apply* as the closing "wow" evidence on "why should we hire you" and as the final thing you leave the manager with.
- **The three purposes of a job interview (diagnostic frame).** An interview exists to assess (1) whether you can do the job (skills, knowledge, qualifications), (2) whether you have the motivation and passion to do the work, and (3) whether you'll fit into the team. *Apply* to reframe any answer around what the hiring manager is actually assessing.
- **"An interview is just a MEETING" (nerves reframe).** "A job interview is simply a meeting between you and a potential employer to find out whether you are suitable for the job role and also whether you want to work for the company." Combine with "start telling yourself you are excited about the interview." *Apply* whenever a candidate reports nerves or anxiety.

### Anti-Patterns (NEVER do these)
- NEVER let a candidate say "my biggest weakness is that I'm a perfectionist" or "I don't have any weaknesses" — these are the classic fails; the weakness question assesses honesty and self-awareness, so give a real weakness plus what you're doing to improve it.
- NEVER answer "How would you deal with conflict with a co-worker?" with "I would tell my manager." "90% of candidates answer that question in that way and they don't get hired." Take ownership and resolve it independently first.
- NEVER say anything negative or disrespectful about a current/former employer, co-worker, or manager (e.g. "I want to leave because there's a toxic work environment"). "If you are negative, you won't get hired" — the manager will think the problem is you.
- NEVER give a single reason (or a generic one) for "Why should we hire you?" — give three (sometimes four) unique reasons and, ideally, hand over a 30-60-90 day plan. Never say "because I really need this job" or "because you pay well."
- NEVER say "No, I don't have any questions, you covered everything" at the end. "That is a missed opportunity" — always ask three smart questions.
- NEVER waffle into hobbies, family, relationship status, or personal problems in "Tell me about yourself." Keep it professional, work-related, ~60 seconds.
- NEVER discuss salary first — avoid it unless they ask "what are your salary expectations?" or they've offered the job without stating pay.
- NEVER give a weak, evidence-free answer ("I'm really good at my job and I'm a strong team worker") — always give evidence and, where possible, numbers of previous strong work performance.

## Detailed Frameworks & Playbooks

### 1. Pre-Interview Preparation (the foundation of every answer)
Do three things before any interview:
1. **Read the job description.** It is "the blueprint for the role you have applied for" and "95% of candidates do not know it exists." It lists the essential skills and qualities — align your strengths and your "why should we hire you" with it.
2. **Research the company (under 5 minutes).** Go to the website "About Us" page (what they do, products/services, mission and values); read positive online customer reviews; and follow them on social media for latest news. A fast method: paste into ChatGPT — *"Give me a quick overview of [company name]: what they do, key products and services, latest news and what people say positively about them."*
3. **Practice your answers** to the most common questions, out loud, in front of a friend, a relative, or a mirror — "this will help the candidate feel the pressure they will be under during the real interview."

**Interview-day basics:** arrive 10–15 minutes early for in-person (log on 5 minutes early online and know the platform); be polite to everyone including the receptionist (managers ask "what was the person like when they arrived?"); wear a smart, professional outfit (best color is **dark blue** — "represents trust and loyalty"); bring two documents — your **CV/resume** and a printed **30-60-90 day plan**.

**Interview technique / body language:** firm handshake ("Hello. Pleased to meet you!"), maintain eye contact (look at the *camera* in online interviews), sit upright with palms resting on top of your knees, smile, use hands only occasionally.

**The three things hiring managers look for:** (1) the right attitude + enthusiasm, (2) the skills and knowledge to do the job competently, (3) strong communication skills (the top skill in every role — hence practice).

### 2. Overcoming Interview Nerves
Nerves are "perfectly normal both before and during a job interview." Two techniques: (1) reframe — "an interview is simply a meeting between you and a potential employer"; (2) tell yourself you are *excited* about the interview — "Try it. It really works!… Your mindset will change." If nerves overwhelm you mid-interview, say: *"I do apologize, I'm not very good at job interviews, but I am very passionate about what I do and I have a strong work ethic. If you do hire me, I will turn up to work early every day, take on board feedback and conduct my duties diligently and professionally to ensure you receive a great return on my salary."*

### 3. Behavioral Questions with No Experience
If you've never been in the situation, "be honest and say to the hiring manager, 'I have never been in that situation before. However, here's exactly what I would do,'" then describe the actions you'd take. For freshers, swap Experience for Educational background in SEAT, and draw achievements from studies, group projects, sports, volunteering, or study-support groups.

### 4. What To Do If You Can't Answer a Question
"Do not worry. It does not mean that you are going to fail the interview." Two options: (1) *"Would it be okay to revisit this question at the end of the interview?"* (buys time; "the chances are they will forget"); (2) *"I am unsure of my answer to that question, however, I will research it once I get home after the interview and I will email you with my response."*

### 5. Questions To Ask At The End ("Do you have any questions?")
Never decline. Ask **three smart questions** that show you want to do great things for the company and avoid time-off/benefits/salary. His rotating set of best questions:
- "What is it that the top-performing employees do in this company to achieve success?"
- "Can you please tell me more about the team I would be a part of in this role and their responsibilities?"
- "What challenges is the team currently facing and what can I do in this role to help overcome them?"
- "What advice would you give to the successful candidate who wants to excel in their work for your company?"
- "What does excellence look like in this position?"
- "What's the best thing about working for this company?"
- "Is there any additional information I can read to learn more about your company while I wait to hear the result of my interview?"

### 6. Closing Statement & Follow-Up
End with a **powerful closing statement** — "the final thing the hiring manager will remember you for": *"Just before I leave, I would like to say thank you very much for giving me the opportunity today to interview for this position. The interview process has made me even more excited about the possibility of working for your company. I look forward to hearing from you in due course. Thank you very much."* Then send a **follow-up email within 24 hours** of the interview finishing.

### 7. Final / Second Interview Extras
At a final interview, expect harder strategy questions ("Based on your research and our previous discussions, what recommendations would you suggest to enhance or improve our company?", "How do you see yourself fitting into our company culture?"). For prioritization, cite a named method — the **Eisenhower Matrix**: urgent+important handled immediately, important-not-urgent scheduled, urgent-not-important delegated, neither minimized/eliminated. (For entry roles he also teaches a **traffic-light system**: red = urgent, amber/blue = second, green = later in the day.)

### 8. Role-Specific Playbook Pattern
Every role tutorial follows the same shape: (1) the questions that "feature during 95% of [role] interviews," (2) two tips per question, (3) a "unique top-scoring example answer" per question, (4) where to download the full pack (e.g. "23 describe-a-time answers," "21 HR generalist answers," "26 department head answers"). He weaves the role's required competencies (e.g. for a store manager: leadership, team management, customer service, commercial awareness) into every answer. For **NHS/healthcare**, learn and demonstrate the six NHS values (working together for patients; respect and dignity; commitment to quality of care; compassion; improving lives; everyone counts) via STAR answers.

### "Tell me about yourself" / "Introduce yourself" — SEAT (generic)
*Structure: SEAT, ~60 seconds.*
> "Thank you for this opportunity. I would describe myself as a disciplined, productive and supportive team player with the skills, qualities and knowledge to excel in this position. My experience in completing time-sensitive tasks, collaborating across company departments and problem-solving means I can add value in the role and give you a great return on my salary. I have a track record of accomplishment. In my last position, I was praised for my strong work ethic, flexibility in my job description, and for how I helped to train new team members. If you hire me, I will be a good company role model and take ownership of my professional development to ensure I am always a useful asset to my team."

I am the type of person who takes ownership of my professional development, supports work colleagues and commits fully to the success of the company. And I look forward to sharing additional details in the interview regarding my suitability for this position."

### "What is your biggest weakness?" (the flagship "safe" weakness)
> "My biggest weakness is that I struggle to say no to people, which means I sometimes overcommit. I have this weakness because I am a people pleaser and don't want to let anyone down. However, I am learning to assess my capacity for additional responsibilities before saying yes, to ensure high-quality work standards are always maintained."

**Other approved weaknesses:**

- *Asking for help:* "In the past, I have been reluctant to ask for help because I prefer to solve problems myself. However, I have learned that collaboration leads to better solutions. ### STAR — "Describe a time you worked on a successful team"
> "In my last role, I worked as part of a team tasked with identifying ways to increase company sales.
